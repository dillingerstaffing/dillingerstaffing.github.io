03 · Exposure register

Change the smoker, not the pack.

Hold one machine-rated cigarette constant and change cigarettes per day, puff intensity, and years. The register computes a comparative smoke workload, not a medical prediction.

The strongest term. Cigarettes per day multiplied by years smoked dominates body impact more than brand livery or a low machine rating.
Why an index, not a dose. Real puff volume, vent blocking, butt length, inhalation depth, metabolism, and dependence are not captured by a single reliable conversion. The index keeps the arithmetic inspectable.
04 · Body pathways

Different constituents, different routes.

A cigarette does not produce one impact score. Nicotine, carbon monoxide, particles, and menthol alter different systems through different mechanisms.

One cigarette, several mechanisms. Machine tar, nicotine, and CO are separate fields because they are separate materials with separate biological routes.
Menthol changes use. Its best-established difference is behavioral: cooling and anesthetic properties make initiation easier and quitting harder. Evidence does not support a separate lung-cancer ranking by menthol status.

07 · Method, revision, and evidence

The measurement is not the person.

In 2008 the FTC rescinded its guidance on machine yields because the test does not meaningfully report the tar and nicotine a smoker receives. The earlier reading remains visible here because correcting it is part of the evidence.

Former reading

Lower machine yield means a proportionally lower human dose.

Retained reading

Machine yield describes a fixed smoking protocol. Human dose also depends on compensation, puff topography, vent blocking, cigarettes per day, butt length, inhalation, metabolism, dependence, and health.
